Target
======

DeepSeek-V4-Flash TP4 MegaMoE on one H20 per rank, M in
{8,16,32,64,128}, prequantized FP8 activation plus MXFP4 W13/W2 weights.
The candidate is one persistent business kernel containing route alignment,
W13, SwiGLU/requantization, W2, local k6 reduction, and CARv2 all-reduce.
The control is the same-source multi-kernel CUDA-Graph path.  Every timed
replay is cold-L2 via an excluded 256 MiB clear.

Bottleneck evidence
===================

The selected M128 launch is 702 x 128 (nine CTAs per each of 78 SMs), 56
registers/thread, no local spilling.  Existing NCU evidence reports only
60.07% compute and 50.73% DRAM throughput, 0.63 issued warps/scheduler/cycle,
36.61% no-eligible cycles, and 37.85% of warp-state cost in barriers.  The
activation loop exit plus terminal W2/grid convergence account for most of
the sampled barrier stalls.  Independent tail mapping measurement showed
the residual W13 and W2 waves are already count-balanced across SMs; generic
task stealing is therefore not the missing optimization.

Hypothesis for this iteration
=============================

Preserve the production WGMMA task order, outlined compact W13 body, 128
threads/CTA, and 8/9-CTA-per-SM occupancy.  At the last W13 wave, publish the
completed full-wave prefix with one generation-tagged, release-sequenced
arrival per CTA.  Tail producers continue without waiting.  Only suffix CTAs
that have no residual W13 task acquire that publication and execute at most
one dependency-safe activation group before the existing final W13 safety
barrier.  This bounds overlap to work likely to fit under the tail, adds no
per-GEMM-task atomics, and never overlaps the cold W13 and W2 weight streams.

Acceptance gate
===============

1. Flag-off cubin/resources and output are unchanged.
2. TP4 all-M and TP8 smoke correctness pass, including repeated CUDA-Graph
   replay generation state.
3. Candidate must improve paired cold-L2 median/GM beyond run noise without
   regressing any large-M endpoint materially; otherwise reject and retain
   the production default.

Screen 1 result and repair
==========================

The first implementation was correct but regressed the one-kernel latency
versus the fresh production anchor by about 9.3%, 5.8%, 3.8%, 2.7%, and 3.0%
at M8/16/32/64/128.  It split the M128 residual W13 task into a second device
call, increasing the M128 stack frame from 32 to 48 bytes, and imposed route
map/readiness work on shapes whose tails were too short to amortize it.

The repaired screen keeps readiness and the residual producer task inside the
original compact W13 phase call, so its external ABI remains one pointer.  It
enables overlap only in the M128 specialization; M<=64 compile back to the
selected production W13/activation flow and do not write the inverse route
map.  This is the final targeted repair before rejecting the approach.

Final result
============

The repaired M128 path remains slower.  In adjacent 40-sample cold-L2 runs,
dependency-ready M128 is 0.356576 ms and flag-off production is 0.349664 ms:
a direct 1.98% regression.  Normalizing each arm by its paired multi-kernel
control still leaves a 1.29% regression.  The candidate retains 56 registers
and no fixed local allocation, but its monolithic entry uses a 48-byte stack
frame versus production's 32 bytes.  M8 and M64, which compile back to the
production schedule, return to 0.075616 and 0.280400 ms respectively.

Decision: reject dependency-ready W13/activation tail overlap as a default.
Keep it default-off as a reproducible falsification artifact.  Do not add the
planned W2/communication chunk overlap: prior flat/chunk screens already show
the chunk reorder/producer-readiness overhead is negative, and this iteration
failed the prerequisite that phase-tail readiness pay for itself.  Production
therefore remains the fixed-phase compact bundle.
